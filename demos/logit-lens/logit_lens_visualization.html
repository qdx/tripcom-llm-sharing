🔬 Logit Lens: Layer-by-Layer Belief Visualization

How GPT-2's "belief" about the next token evolves across transformer layers — Direct vs Chain-of-Thought

What is the Logit Lens?

The Logit Lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) is an interpretability technique that reveals what a transformer "believes" at intermediate layers. At each layer, we project the residual stream through the final unembedding matrix to get a probability distribution over tokens — as if the model were forced to predict right now from that layer's representation.

This lets us watch the model's "thinking" unfold: does it know the answer early? Does it gradually build confidence? Or does it make a sudden leap in the final layers?

Key finding: With Chain-of-Thought prompting, supporting reasoning tokens build up the right representation gradually. With Direct prompting, the model must make a more uncertain "jump" in the final layers — relying on compressed internal computation rather than explicit reasoning steps.

📝 Presentation Talking Points

  • Early layers (L0–L3): Both Direct and CoT are uncertain — the model hasn't "decided" yet. Probability is near zero for the target token.
  • Middle layers (L4–L8): CoT prompts show earlier and smoother convergence toward the target token. The reasoning context provides stepping stones for the residual stream.
  • Final layers (L9–L11): Direct prompts often show a sharp probability jump — the model "scrambles" to find the answer without intermediate support.
  • Entropy: CoT consistently has lower entropy in later layers = the model is more "confident" and less scattered across the vocabulary.
  • Why this matters: This is direct evidence that CoT isn't just a prompt trick — it fundamentally changes the internal computation trajectory of the model.
